Keeping Your Streamwood Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Streamwood Homeowners Face

Most water heater problems we see in Streamwood come back to what's in the water. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be a real factor: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Code requirements add another layer. Swapping out an older heater often means updating the installation along the way: a thermal expansion tank if the water system is closed, a relief valve drain line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Well water brings its own challenges, including heavier sediment, iron staining, and the sulfur odor that occasionally shows up when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a quick diagnostic visit catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
